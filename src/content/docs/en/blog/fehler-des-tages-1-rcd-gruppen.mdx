## The situation

New Unterverteilung (sub-distribution board), two RCD groups, everything neatly labeled. Erstprüfung (initial verification before first commissioning) passed – or so everyone thought.
Then the customer switches on the washing machine and **RCD 2 trips**. Sometimes both.
No short circuit, no fault current, no faulty appliance. And still: off.

## What happened

An RCD compares the Strom (current) flowing out through **L** with the Strom coming back through **N**.
If both are equal, everything is fine. If the sum deviates by more than the rated residual current (30 mA), it switches off.

The mistake: the Außenleiter (line conductor / phase) of the washing machine is connected to **RCD 1**, but its Neutralleiter (neutral conductor) was screwed to the **N terminal of RCD 2**.
As long as no Strom flows, nobody notices anything. As soon as the machine runs, Strom flows in through L via RCD 1 –
and back through N via RCD 2. For RCD 1 the return Strom is "missing", for RCD 2 Strom "arrives" that never flowed out.
Both see a difference. At least one of them trips.

## How it should look

Every Endstromkreis (final circuit) belongs **completely** to one RCD group: L via the LS-Schalter (miniature circuit breaker, MCB) of that group, **N to the N terminal
of the same group**. The N terminals of the two groups must never be connected.

Rule of thumb in the distribution board: **"L and N go for a walk together."** Wherever the Außenleiter comes from, that's where the Neutralleiter goes.

## How you find it in 30 seconds

1. Switch off all circuits of the tripping group.
2. Switch loads on one by one – the circuit that makes it trip is the suspect.
3. Trace the Neutralleiter of that circuit in the distribution board: does it end at the N terminal of the **other** group? Found it.
