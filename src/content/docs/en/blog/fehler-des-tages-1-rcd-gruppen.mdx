## The situation

New Unterverteilung (sub-distribution board), two RCD (residual current device) groups, everything neatly labeled. Erstprüfung (initial verification) passed – or so you thought.
Then the customer turns on the washing machine and **RCD 2 trips**. Sometimes both.
No Kurzschluss (short circuit), no Fehlerstrom (residual current), no faulty appliance. Still: it trips.

## What happened

An RCD compares the Strom (current) flowing in via the Außenleiter (phase conductor) with the Strom returning via the Neutralleiter (neutral conductor).
If both are equal, everything is fine. If the sum deviates by more than the Bemessungsdifferenzstrom (rated residual operating current) (30 mA), it switches off.

The error: The Außenleiter of the washing machine is connected to **RCD 1**, but its Neutralleiter was screwed into the **N-Klemme (N-terminal) of RCD 2**. As long as no Strom is flowing, nobody notices anything. As soon as the machine runs, Strom flows in via L through RCD 1 – and returns via N through RCD 2. For RCD 1, the return Strom is "missing"; for RCD 2, Strom "arrives" that never flowed out through it.
Both see a difference. At least one trips.

## What it should look like

Every Endstromkreis (final circuit) belongs **completely** to one RCD group: L via the LS-Schalter (miniature circuit breaker) of that group, **N to the N-Klemme of the same group**. The N-Klemmen of the two groups must never be connected.

Rule of thumb in the distribution board: **"L and N go for a walk together."** Where the Außenleiter comes from, the Neutralleiter must go.

## How to find it in 30 seconds

1. Switch off all circuits of the tripping group.
2. Switch on appliances one by one – the circuit that causes the trip is the suspicious one.
3. Trace the Neutralleiter of this circuit in the distribution board: Does it land on the N-Klemme of the **other** group? Found it.

Before touching anything in the distribution board: [fünf Sicherheitsregeln](../../grundlagen/sicherheitsregeln/). The entire distribution board step by step: [Unterverteilung aufbauen](../../themen/energie-und-gebaeudetechnik/unterverteilung/).
