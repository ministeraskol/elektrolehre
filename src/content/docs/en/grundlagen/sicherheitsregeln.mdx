## Why Strom (current) is so dangerous

You cannot see, hear, or smell Strom. From about **10 mA**, muscles cramp – the hand can no longer let go of the conductor. From about **30 mA**, there is a risk of **ventricular fibrillation**, after just a few hundred milliseconds. For comparison: an LED lamp draws 35 mA. The body resistance with dry skin is about 1 000 Ω – at 230 V Spannung (voltage), this means over 200 mA flows. That is why the order of the five rules is not a ritual, but a survival strategy.

Additionally, there is the **arc fault**: In the event of a short circuit with a tool on a busbar, temperatures of several thousand degrees occur – causing severe burns even without Strom flowing through the body.

## The five safety rules – step by step

The rules originate from **DIN VDE 0105-100** and apply before any work on an electrical system, in a **de-energized state**. Follow the order – when setting up, go from 1 to 5; when switching back on, go backwards from 5 to 1.

### 1. Disconnect

Switch off the system or the part of the system **all-pole**: LS-Schalter (miniature circuit breaker) off, RCD off, Hauptschalter (main switch) off, if necessary SLS off, remove the fuse. "All-pole" means: the neutral conductor must also be disconnected where intended. Just switching off the light switch is **not** disconnecting.

### 2. Secure against reconnection

Someone else must not be able to switch it back on accidentally. Means: **locking element** on the LS-Schalter, **padlock** on the Hauptschalter, take the removed fuse **with you**, **prohibition sign** "Do not switch – work in progress". A note is not a safety measure. In foreign buildings: inform whoever else has access.

### 3. Verify absence of Spannung

Use a **two-pole voltage tester** (according to DIN EN 61243-3) to check **all** conductors: L1, L2, L3 against N, against PE, and against each other. Before and after, test the tester on a **known Spannung** – a defective tester will not show anything even on a system under Spannung. Phase tester screwdrivers ("lying pens") are **not approved** for this.

### 4. Ground and short-circuit

Connect all conductors to the ground and to each other. If the system should become energized – due to back-feeding, incorrect switching, photovoltaics – the protective device will trip immediately. In **low voltage** (up to 1 000 V), this rule is only necessary if back-feeding is possible (PV system, emergency power, overhead line) – usually not in residential installations. In high voltage, it is always mandatory.

### 5. Cover or barrier adjacent parts under Spannung

What could not be disconnected – the Zuleitung (supply line) before the Hauptschalter, the busbar in the Zählerschrank (meter cabinet) – is covered with **insulating covers** (cloths, molded parts, protective plates) or made inaccessible by barriers.

Only when all five steps are completed does the work begin. When leaving and returning: repeat step 3.

## Working under Spannung

Principle: **Work is not performed under Spannung.** Exceptions (DIN VDE 0105-100) require special training (AuS), special tools and protective equipment, and written instructions. For an Azubi (apprentice), the rule is: **never.** Even "quickly swapping" a Steckdose (socket) under Spannung is prohibited and is considered a serious error during Ausbildung (apprenticeship).

Only **measuring and testing** with suitable devices (measurement category CAT III for distribution boards, CAT IV for Hausanschluss (building connection)) and **operating** are allowed under Spannung.

## Who is allowed to do what? Elektrofachkraft, EuP, layperson

**DGUV Vorschrift 3** and DIN VDE 0105-100 distinguish between three groups of people:

| Person | Definition | Allowed to |
|---|---|---|
| **Elektrofachkraft (EFK)** (qualified electrician) | completed Ausbildung in an electrical profession (or equivalent) plus experience and knowledge of standards | work independently on electrical systems, install, test, and release systems |
| **Elektrotechnisch unterwiesene Person (EuP)** (electrotechnically instructed person) | instructed by an EFK for specific, recurring tasks | only these tasks, under the direction and supervision of the EFK |
| **Elektrotechnischer Laie** (electrotechnical layperson) | everyone else | operate devices, switch the fuse back on, press the test button on the RCD – **not** connect wires, swap Steckdosen, open distribution boards |

The **Azubi** is not an Elektrofachkraft – only after receiving the journeyman's certificate. During Ausbildung, you work **under supervision** of an EFK, who bears the responsibility. This is not a lack of trust, but the law.

### The responsible Elektrofachkraft (vEFK)

In companies, a **responsible Elektrofachkraft** bears the technical and supervisory responsibility: They determine who is allowed to do what and organize tests and instructions. In a craft business, this is usually the Meister (master craftsman).

## § 13 NAV – the boundary to the grid operator

The **Niederspannungsanschlussverordnung** (NAV) regulates the relationship between the grid operator and the connection user. § 13 states: Work on the electrical system **behind the Hausanschluss** may only be carried out by a company that is registered in the **installer directory of a grid operator**. This concerns:

- **Hausanschlusskasten (HAK)** (building connection box) and main line
- **Zählerplatz** (meter location): setting up the Zählerschrank, installing the SLS, wiring the meter panel
- **Commissioning** of new systems and registration with the grid operator
- Registration of **PV systems, wallboxes, heat pumps**

The **meter itself** is installed and **sealed** by the grid operator or metering point operator. Breaking seals is prohibited – even for the electrical company, except after consultation.

Only a company with a **responsible Elektrofachkraft with Meister qualification** (or equivalent) can be included in the installer directory – the actual reason why the Meister in the electrical trade is so in demand.

## Personal protective equipment and tools

- **Insulated tools** (VDE tool, 1 000 V tested, double triangle symbol).
- **Two-pole voltage tester**, regularly tested.
- **Safety goggles** when working on distribution boards and busbars (arc fault).
- **Safety shoes** on construction sites; helmet if necessary.
- No rings, watches, chains – no metal on the body.
