## From the power plant to the Steckdose

Electricity is generated and distributed as **Drehstrom** (three-phase AC): three alternating voltages shifted by 120° relative to each other. They enter the house via three **Außenleiter** (phase conductors) L1, L2, L3, plus the **Neutralleiter** (neutral conductor) N and – for safety – the **Schutzleiter** (protective earth conductor) PE.

This results in the two voltages of the domestic power supply:

| Between | Spannung (voltage) | Name | Typical loads |
|---|---|---|---|
| L1 and N (or L2–N, L3–N) | **230 V** | AC, phase voltage | Steckdose (socket-outlet), light, all household appliances with a plug |
| L1 and L2 (or L2–L3, L3–L1) | **400 V** | Drehstrom, line-to-line voltage | Stove, instantaneous water heater, 11 kW wallbox, heat pump, motors |

The frequency throughout Europe is **50 Hz**: The Spannung changes direction 50 times per second. The "230 V" is the **RMS value**; the peak value is approximately 325 V.

## The five conductors and their colors

Since DIN VDE 0293-308 (2003), colors in Germany have been standardized. You must know them by heart – the color determines whether an installation is safe.

| Conductor | Abbreviation | Color | Task |
|---|---|---|---|
| Außenleiter 1 | **L1** | **brown** | carries Spannung ("phase") |
| Außenleiter 2 | **L2** | **black** | carries Spannung |
| Außenleiter 3 | **L3** | **gray** | carries Spannung |
| Neutralleiter | **N** | **blue** | return conductor, in normal operation close to 0 V against earth |
| Schutzleiter | **PE** | **green-yellow** | carries no Strom (current) in normal operation; conducts fault current to earth so that the protective device switches off |

**Green-yellow is taboo for everything else.** The Schutzleiter must never be used as an Außenleiter or Neutralleiter, and no other conductor may be green-yellow. **Blue** is reserved for the Neutralleiter; only if no N is present (for example in a switch circuit), may blue be used differently – then the core must be marked at both ends. It is cleaner not to do it at all.

**Black and gray** in a 3-core cable: If you see a cable with brown, black, gray in a bathroom, it is a switch or Wechselschaltung (two-way circuit) cable without N – brown is the incoming Außenleiter, black and gray are switched or corresponding.

### Number of cores and assignment of common cables (NYM-J)

| Cable | Cores | Usage |
|---|---|---|
| NYM-J 3×1.5 mm² | brown, blue, green-yellow | Light (B10) |
| NYM-J 3×2.5 mm² | brown, blue, green-yellow | Steckdose (B16) |
| NYM-J 5×1.5 mm² | brown, black, gray, blue, green-yellow | Wechselschaltung with Steckdose, series circuit |
| NYM-J 5×2.5 mm² | brown, black, gray, blue, green-yellow | Stove (3×B16, 400 V) |
| NYM-J 5×10 mm² / 5×16 mm² | as above | Zuleitung (supply line) Unterverteilung (sub-distribution board), main line |

The **J** means: with green-yellow Schutzleiter. **O** (NYM-O) would have none – practically no longer common in residential construction.

### Older installations

In installations before 2003, you will find **black, brown, black** for the Außenleiter; before about 1965, the Schutzleiter was partly **red** and the Neutralleiter **gray**. In very old installations, there is the **classical protective multiple earthing** without a separate PE. When working in old installations, the rule is: **never rely on the color, always measure.**

## System earthing arrangements: TN, TT, IT

The system earthing arrangement describes how the star point of the transformer and the housings of the devices are connected to earth. The letters (DIN VDE 0100-100):

- First letter – star point of the grid: **T** = directly earthed (terre), **I** = isolated.
- Second letter – exposed conductive parts of the devices: **N** = connected to the star point (via PE/PEN), **T** = own earth electrode.

| System | How | Where |
|---|---|---|
| **TN-C** | PE and N in one conductor (**PEN**) to the load | old installations, main lines, no longer for new Endstromkreise (final circuits) |
| **TN-S** | PE and N separated from the transformer onwards | industry, large installations |
| **TN-C-S** | PEN into the house, there split into PE and N | **Standard in German residential buildings** |
| **TT** | House has its own earth electrode, no PE connection to the grid | some rural grids, Southern Germany, abroad |
| **IT** | Grid not earthed, first fault only triggers a signal | operating theaters, emergency power supply |

In the **TN-C-S-Netz**, the PEN conductor arrives at the **Hausanschlusskasten** (service entrance box) and is split into PE and N on the **main earthing busbar**. From there, the two must never be connected again – otherwise no RCD works, and the Schutzleiter carries operating Strom. That is exactly why every RCD group in the [Unterverteilung](../../themen/energie-und-gebaeudetechnik/unterverteilung/) has its own N-terminal.

## Earthing and equipotential bonding

The **PE** is only as good as the earth it is connected to. This is ensured by the **foundation earth electrode** (a strip steel in the foundation, mandatory for new buildings since 2007), the **main earthing busbar** (HES) in the service room and the **protective equipotential bonding**: water pipes, heating, gas pipe, antenna mast, steel structures are connected to the HES so that in the event of a fault, no Spannung arises between two touchable metal parts.

In the **bathroom**, the **supplementary protective equipotential bonding** is added (DIN VDE 0100-701): bathtub, pipes and PE of the circuits are connected locally.
