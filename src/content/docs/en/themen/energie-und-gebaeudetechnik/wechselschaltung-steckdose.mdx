## The three basic circuits

Almost every residential installation consists of three components that you will wire during your first year of apprenticeship until you can do it in your sleep:

| Circuit | Task | Switch | Wires to the light |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Ausschaltung** (single-pole switching) | switch a light from **one** location | 1 Ausschalter (2 terminals: L, switched conductor) | L (switched), N, PE |
| **Wechselschaltung** (two-way switching) | switch a light from **two** locations (hallway, bedroom) | 2 Wechselschalter (3 terminals each: L, two travelers) | as above; 2 travelers between switches |
| **Steckdose** (socket outlet) | connection of portable devices | none | L, N, PE |

For **three or more** switching locations, the **Kreuzschaltung** (intermediate switching) is added (one Kreuzschalter between two Wechselschalter) – a separate guide will follow.

## Ausschaltung

The simplest circuit. The Außenleiter (phase conductor) **L** (brown) goes to the switch, from the switch the **geschalteter Leiter** (switched conductor) (black) goes to the light, and from the light the **Neutralleiter** (neutral conductor) **N** (blue) returns directly. **Schutzleiter** (protective earth) **PE** (green-yellow) goes to the light if it has a protective earth connection – and always to the box.

Rule number one of installation: **Always switch the Außenleiter, never the N.** If the N is switched, the lamp will still light up, but it will remain live when "off" – life-threatening when changing a bulb.

Cables: **NYM-J 3×1.5 mm²** from the junction box to the switch (brown = L in, black = switched out, carry PE, blue remains unused and insulated or is used as N for a Steckdose next to the switch) and **3×1.5 mm²** to the light.

## Wechselschaltung

<WechselschaltungSchema />

Two **Wechselschalter** each have three terminals: **L** (common contact, often marked with an arrow) and **two outputs**. The switch connects L to either one or the other output.

- **Switch 1:** L (brown) to terminal **L**. Both outputs go as **korrespondierende Leiter** (travelers) (black and gray) to Switch 2.
- **Switch 2:** travelers to both outputs. From terminal **L**, the **geschalteter Leiter** (black) goes to the light.
- **Light:** switched conductor, N, PE.

Regardless of the position: A switch always changes the state. If both switches are on the same traveler, the circuit is closed – light on.

Cables: **NYM-J 5×1.5 mm²** between the switches (brown L, black and gray travelers, blue N for a Steckdose, PE) or 3×1.5 mm² if there is no Steckdose at the switch.

**Check the terminal L on the switch with a continuity tester** before you connect it – manufacturers mark them differently. If you swap L and a traveler connection, the circuit will only work in one position.

## Steckdose (Schutzkontakt)

The **Schuko-Steckdose** (earthed socket) has three terminals: **L**, **N** and **PE** (protective contact, the two clips). In Germany, the Steckdose is not polarity-protected – L can be on the left or right. Common practice (not a standard) is **N left, L right** when looking at the socket.

Rules:

- **Always connect PE** – even if the device only has two poles. A Steckdose without PE is a defect.
- **Every Steckdose in the apartment must be behind an RCD 30 mA** (DIN VDE 0100-410).
- **B16** with **2.5 mm²**. 1.5 mm² with B16 only for short cable runs (up to approx. 15 m when installed under plaster) – vocational school will calculate this.
- **Push-in terminals** for modern sockets: strip wire **10–12 mm**, insert straight, **pull test**. For screw terminals: torque according to manufacturer specifications.
- Sockets in the **bathroom**: observe protection zones according to DIN VDE 0100-701 – distance of 60 cm from the bathtub / shower.
- **Minimum equipment** according to DIN 18015-2: for example, living room up to 20 m² at least 5 sockets, kitchen at least 7 (3 of which above the worktop). If you plan too few, everything will end up on power strips later.

## Wiring step by step (training wall or apartment)

<Steps>

1. **Five safety rules.** LS (circuit breaker) off, locking element, check for absence of voltage at the junction box and all boxes using a two-pole tester.

2. **Read the plan.** Circuit diagram in **exploded view** (functional relationship) and **installation plan** (where is what). Check off wires and terminals on the plan before you connect them.

3. **Pull in cables.** Star-shaped from the junction box: to Switch 1, Switch 2, light, Steckdose. Cut the cable with 15–20 cm reserve in each box. Cable routing in **installation zones** (horizontally 30 cm below the ceiling or above the floor, vertically 15 cm next to doors and corners).

4. **Strip the sheath and insulation.** Sheath without nicks in the wires, strip wire insulation **10–12 mm** (observe terminal specifications). Do not damage any copper strands.

5. **Connect the junction box.** With **connecting terminals** (Wago 221 or push-in terminals): all N together, all PE together, L to the outgoing L-wire to Switch 1, connect travelers through, switched conductor to the light. **No wire may lie loose in the box.**

6. **Connect switch and Steckdose.** Terminals according to the imprint. Confirm L on the Wechselschalter with a continuity tester. PE to the Steckdose first.

7. **Connect the light.** Switched conductor to L of the light, N to N, PE to PE. Even for protection class II lights, keep PE ready in the box (insulate it).

8. **Test before switching on.** See next section.

9. **Switch on and test function.** Test the Wechselschaltung from all four combinations. Test the Steckdose with a socket tester.

10. **Label.** Enter the circuit in the Zählerplatz (meter panel) or Unterverteilung (sub-distribution board).

</Steps>

## Testing according to DIN VDE 0100-600

Even the smallest circuit is tested and documented:

- **Visual inspection:** colors correct, PE everywhere, no damaged wires, boxes closed, covers in place.
- **Continuity of the protective conductor:** from the PE terminal in the distribution board to the protective contact of the Steckdose and to the PE of the light, low-resistance (typically below 1 Ω).
- **Insulation resistance:** all conductors against PE, 500 V DC, at least **1 MΩ**. Disconnect lamps and devices or unscrew them beforehand.
- **Loop impedance** at the Steckdose: the LS must trip in the event of a short circuit (B16: short-circuit current at least 80 A).
- **RCD test:** tripping time and tripping current at the Steckdose.
- **Phase rotation** does not apply to 230 V.

## Common mistakes

- **N switched instead of L.** Works, but is life-threatening.
- **L and traveler connection on the Wechselschalter swapped.** Circuit only works "sometimes".
- **PE not connected** or used as L in the box.
- **Blue wire used as switched conductor** without marking.
- **Wire stripped too short** – terminal grips the insulation: loose contact, heat.
- **Wire stripped too long** – bare copper outside the terminal: risk of electric shock, short circuit.
