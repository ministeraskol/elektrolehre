## After the Gesellenbrief

Once you have passed your Gesellenprüfung (journeyman examination), you are a **Elektroniker/-in Fachrichtung Energie- und Gebäudetechnik** (electronics technician for energy and building technology) and at **Level 4** in the **Deutscher Qualifikationsrahmen (DQR)** (German Qualifications Framework). From here, three major paths lead onward: **Meister**, **Techniker** or **Studium** (university studies). All three are possible without the Abitur – that is the strength of the German system.

| Path | Qualification | DQR | Typical Duration | What It Gets You |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Meisterprüfung** | Elektrotechnikermeister/-in, plus „Bachelor Professional in Elektrotechnik" | 6 | 1 year full-time or approx. 2 years part-time alongside work | Found and run your own business, train Azubis, entry in the Installateurverzeichnis |
| **Fachschule** | Staatlich geprüfte/-r Techniker/-in Elektrotechnik | 6 | 2 years full-time or 4 years part-time | Planning, project management, site management, industry |
| **Studium** | Bachelor of Engineering (Elektrotechnik, Gebäudetechnik) | 6 | 3–3½ years | Development, planning office, engineering career |

Meister, Techniker and Bachelor all sit at DQR Level 6. That means **gleichwertig** (equivalent), not identical: the Meister is the practitioner and entrepreneur, the Techniker the planner, the engineer the developer.

## The Meister

The Meisterprüfung (master craftsman examination) is governed by § 45 of the **Handwerksordnung (HwO)** (Trade and Crafts Code). It has **four parts**:

1. **Part I – Fachpraxis (practical skills):** a Meisterprüfungsprojekt (master project, for example planning and executing a complete building installation) with a technical discussion and a situational task.
2. **Part II – Fachtheorie (theory):** Elektrotechnik, Anlagen- und Systemtechnik, Auftragsabwicklung, Betriebsführung.
3. **Part III – Betriebswirtschaft, Kaufmännisches und Recht (business administration, commercial matters and law).**
4. **Part IV – Berufs- und Arbeitspädagogik** (vocational and work pedagogy, i.e. trainer aptitude, AEVO).

Anyone who has passed the Gesellenprüfung in the Elektrotechniker-Handwerk is admitted; there has been no waiting period since 2004. If you pass the exam, you may additionally call yourself **„Bachelor Professional in Elektrotechnik"** (§ 51 Abs. 2 HwO) – not an academic degree, but the official classification at Bachelor level.

**Why the Meister is especially important in the Elektrohandwerk:** The Elektrotechniker-Handwerk is **zulassungspflichtig** (subject to mandatory registration, Anlage A HwO). Anyone who wants to run an electrical business independently needs the Meister or an equivalent qualification. And only a registered business may work on the Hausanschluss (house connection) and on the Zähler (meter) (§ 13 NAV, Installateurverzeichnis of the grid operator).

Preparation costs money (courses, exam fees, materials). The **Aufstiegs-BAföG** (AFBG) funds Meister and Techniker courses with a grant and a loan; after passing the exam, part of the loan is forgiven. The responsible authority in your Bundesland (federal state) provides information.

## The Staatlich geprüfter Techniker

The **Fachschule für Technik** (technical school, specializing in Elektrotechnik, often with a focus on Energie- und Gebäudetechnik or automation) teaches in **two years of full-time study** (or four years part-time alongside work) what the Ausbildung (apprenticeship) covers only briefly: planning, calculation, project management, standards in depth. The requirement is the Gesellenbrief (journeyman certificate) plus, as a rule, **one year of work experience**.

With the Techniker qualification, you also receive the **Fachhochschulreife** (university of applied sciences entrance qualification) in most Bundesländer. Techniker work in planning offices, in site management, in industry, at grid operators – and usually earn significantly more than Gesellen.

## Studium Without the Abitur

Since the KMK resolution of **6 March 2009**, the following applies nationwide:

- **Meister and Techniker** have the **allgemeine Hochschulzugangsberechtigung** (general higher education entrance qualification) – they may study any subject at any university.
- **Gesellen with at least three years of work experience** receive a **fachgebundene Hochschulzugangsberechtigung** (subject-restricted higher education entrance qualification) for a subject that matches their profession (for example Elektrotechnik, Gebäudetechnik) – usually after an aptitude interview or an examination.

Popular degree programs after the Ausbildung: **Elektrotechnik**, **Energie- und Gebäudetechnik**, **Gebäudeautomation**, **Regenerative Energien**. Many universities offer them part-time alongside work or as dual programs.

## Specializations Without a New Qualification

Not everyone wants to become a Meister. In the Elektrohandwerk there are in-demand specializations that run through manufacturer courses or certificates:

- **Photovoltaik und Speicher** (photovoltaics and battery storage) – planning, installation, grid connection, registration with the Netzbetreiber (grid operator).
- **Ladeinfrastruktur (Wallbox, Ladeparks)** (charging infrastructure) – load management, billing, grid registration.
- **Gebäudeautomation (KNX-Partner, Smart Home)** (building automation) – certification by the KNX Association.
- **Prüfen ortsveränderlicher und ortsfester Anlagen** (testing portable and fixed installations) – DGUV Vorschrift 3, DIN VDE 0701/0702, as a „verantwortliche Elektrofachkraft" (responsible electrical specialist).
- **Wärmepumpe und Sektorkopplung** (heat pump and sector coupling) – working together with SHK-Betrieben (plumbing and HVAC companies).
- **Brandmelde- und Sicherheitstechnik** (fire alarm and security technology) – Fachkraft according to DIN 14675.

Every one of these directions is in such demand today that companies usually pay for the training.

## How to Decide

- **You want your own jobs, your own business, responsibility for people?** → Meister.
- **You want to plan and calculate, less construction site, more office?** → Techniker.
- **You want to develop, do research, go into industry or an engineering office?** → Studium.
- **You want to earn money first and get really good?** → two to three years as a Geselle, then a specialization. Many Meister started exactly like that.

No path is final. Techniker later do the Meister, Meister study part-time alongside work. The Gesellenbrief is the key – the rest is time and willpower.
