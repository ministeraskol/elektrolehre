## What is an Unterverteilung?

The **Unterverteilung** (sub-distribution board) (also **Stromkreisverteiler**) is the "fuse box" of an apartment or floor. It receives its Energie (energy) via the **Zuleitung** (supply line) from the **Zählerschrank** (meter cabinet) and distributes it to the individual **Endstromkreise** (final circuits): sockets, lighting, stove, washing machine.

In the Zählerschrank, you’ll find the **Hausanschluss** (house connection), the **SLS-Schalter** (selective main line protection switch) and the meter. Everything behind it – from the Zuleitung onwards – is the customer’s installation. This is exactly where you, as an Azubi (apprentice), will work most often.

The rules for residential buildings are found in **DIN 18015** (planning and minimum equipment) and in the **DIN VDE 0100** standard series (installation of low-voltage systems).

## Components at a Glance

| Component | Function | Typical Selection |
|---|---|---|
| **Hauptschalter** (main switch) | Disconnects the entire distribution board on all poles | 4-pole, 63 A |
| **Überspannungsschutz** (surge protection) (SPD) | Diverts surges caused by lightning or switching operations | Type 2, directly behind the Hauptschalter |
| **RCD / FI-Schutzschalter** | Trips on fault current – protection against electric shock | 4-pole, 40 A or 63 A, IΔn = 30 mA, Type A |
| **LS-Schalter** (circuit breaker) (**Leitungsschutzschalter**) | Protects the line against overload and short circuit | B16 sockets, B10 lighting, B16 3-pole stove |
| **Kammschiene** (comb bar) / Phasenschiene | Connects the LS-Schalter of a group to the RCD | 3-phase, 10 mm² or 16 mm² |
| **N- and PE-Klemmen** | Collect the Neutralleiter (neutral conductor) per RCD group and all Schutzleiter (protective conductor) | Series terminals, N-separating terminals |
| **Hutschiene** (hat rail) | Supports all DIN rail devices | 35 mm; 1 division unit (TE) = 18 mm |
| **Touch protection cover** | Covers the terminals, only the operating levers remain visible | Matching the distribution board |
| **Label strips** | Circuit directory: which switch controls what | Printed, permanent |

## Planning: Circuits and Groups

Before you turn a single screw, you plan on paper:

1. **Count the circuits.** DIN 18015-2 specifies a minimum equipment requirement. For example, **stove**, **washing machine**, **dishwasher** and **tumble dryer** need their own circuits. Sockets and lighting are divided by room.
2. **Plan for reserves.** Rule of thumb: 20 to 30 % of the division units should remain free. A full distribution board becomes a problem during the next renovation.
3. **Observe RCD requirements.** DIN VDE 0100-410 requires an RCD with **IΔn ≤ 30 mA** for **sockets up to 32 A** and for **lighting circuits in apartments**. In practice, almost every Endstromkreis is now connected behind an RCD.
4. **Form groups.** Several LS-Schalter share one RCD. Distribute wisely: lighting and sockets of the same room on **different** RCDs – if one trips, the lights stay on. Six LS-Schalter per RCD is a proven upper limit.
5. **Distribute the Außenleiter** (live conductors). Load L1, L2 and L3 as evenly as possible – the Kammschiene makes this almost automatic.
6. **Check the Vorsicherung** (pre-fuse). The SLS in the Zählerschrank (for example 35 A) must match the RCD (for example 40 A) so that the RCD is never overloaded.

## Assembly Step by Step

<Steps>

1. **Apply the five safety rules.** Disconnect, secure against re-energization, verify absence of voltage, earth and short-circuit, cover adjacent live parts. Always – even for a "quick" look.

2. **Mount the distribution board.** At a comfortable operating height (usually between approx. 1.1 and 1.8 m), freely accessible, dry, not behind doors or furniture.

3. **Introduce the Zuleitung.** Remove the sheath, cut the conductors to length. Fine-stranded conductors get **Aderendhülsen** (wire end sleeves). The cross-section depends on Strom (current), length, and installation method – NYM-J 5×10 mm² is common with a 35 A Vorsicherung, but you calculate it every time.

4. **Install the Hauptschalter and SPD.** Zuleitung to the Hauptschalter, the Überspannungsschutz behind it. Its connection lines to L, N and PE as short as possible, total under 0.5 m.

5. **Install RCDs and feed them.** From the Hauptschalter to each RCD: L1, L2, L3 and N. The N goes **through** the RCD – only then can it detect fault currents.

6. **Connect LS-Schalter with Kammschiene.** One Kammschiene per RCD group from the RCD output to the LS-Schalter. Cut the bar cleanly, no bare ends.

7. **Clamp the outgoing lines.** Außenleiter to the LS output. Neutralleiter to the N-terminal of the **same** RCD group. Schutzleiter to the PE terminal. Colors: **L1 brown, L2 black, L3 grey, N blue, PE green-yellow**.

8. **Tighten and check.** Torque according to manufacturer’s specification (often around 2 Nm for DIN rail devices), then a pull test on each conductor.

9. **Label.** Each switch gets its circuit on the label strip; the circuit directory goes into the door of the distribution board.

10. **Perform the Erstprüfung** (initial inspection) – see next section. Without a Prüfprotokoll (test protocol), the installation is not finished.

</Steps>

## Erstprüfung according to DIN VDE 0100-600

Every new or modified installation is tested before commissioning. The **Erstprüfung** has three parts:

- **Inspect:** correct components, colors, labeling, covers, cable routing, no damage.
- **Test:** function of protective devices, test button on the RCD, switching functions.
- **Measure:**
  - **Continuity of the Schutzleiter** – each PE is connected with low resistance.
  - **Insulation resistance** – measured with 500 V DC, at least **1 MΩ** for installations up to 500 V.
  - **Loop impedance / short-circuit current** – the LS-Schalter must trip reliably in case of a fault.
  - **RCD tripping** – at IΔn no later than **300 ms**; good devices trip significantly faster. Tripping current and tripping time go into the protocol.
  - **Phase sequence** – clockwise at the stove connection.

The result is a **Prüfprotokoll** with measured values, date, and signature. It belongs to the handover.

## Common Mistakes

- **N-conductor of two RCD groups connected.** The RCD sees a differential current and trips under load. The most common mistake in the Ausbildung.
- **Schutzleiter forgotten or loose.** Life-threatening – and immediately visible in the measurement.
- **Wrong characteristic or rated current.** B16 on 1.5 mm² over long distances might be too much; B10 for lighting is the norm.
- **Kammschiene cut incorrectly.** Bare ends next to the neighboring device are a short circuit waiting to happen.
- **Terminals not tightened.** Loose terminals get hot – fire hazard. Pull test on each conductor.
- **No labeling, no protocol.** Without both, the installation is a black box for the next electrician.
