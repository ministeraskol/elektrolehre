## What is a Unterverteilung?

The **Unterverteilung** (sub-distribution board, also known as **Stromkreisverteiler** (circuit distribution board)) is the "fuse box" of an apartment or floor. It receives its **Energie** (energy) via the **Zuleitung** (supply line) from the **Zählerschrank** (meter cabinet) and distributes it to the individual **Endstromkreise** (final circuits): sockets, lighting, stove, washing machine.

Inside the Zählerschrank, you will find the **Hausanschluss** (building service connection), the **SLS-Schalter** (selective main circuit breaker), and the **Zähler** (meter). Everything beyond that – starting from the Zuleitung – is the customer’s installation. This is exactly where you, as an **Azubi** (apprentice), will work most often.

The rules for residential buildings are defined in **DIN 18015** (planning and minimum equipment) and the **DIN VDE 0100** (erection of low-voltage installations) series of standards.

## Overview of components

| Component | Task | Typical selection |
|---|---|---|
| **Hauptschalter** (main switch) | Disconnects the entire distribution board on all poles | 4-pole, 63 A |
| **Überspannungsschutz** (surge protection) | Diverts surges caused by lightning or switching operations | Type 2, directly after the Hauptschalter |
| **RCD** (residual current device) | Disconnects in case of **Fehlerstrom** (residual current) – protection against electric shock | 4-pole, 40 A or 63 A, **Bemessungsdifferenzstrom** (rated residual current) = 30 mA, Type A |
| **LS-Schalter** (miniature circuit breaker) | Protects the line against **Überlast** (overload) and **Kurzschluss** (short circuit) | B16 for sockets, B10 for lighting, B16 3-pole for stove |
| **Kammschiene** (busbar) | Connects the LS-Schalter of a group to the RCD | 3-phase, 10 mm² or 16 mm² |
| **N- and PE-terminals** | Collect the **Neutralleiter** (neutral conductor) per RCD group and all **Schutzleiter** (protective earth conductor) | Terminal blocks, N-disconnect terminals |
| **Hutschiene** (DIN rail) | Holds all modular installation devices | 35 mm; 1 pitch unit (TE) = 18 mm |
| **Touch protection cover** | Covers the terminals, leaving only the switch levers visible | Matching the distribution board |
| **Labeling strips** | Circuit directory: which switch controls what | Printed, permanent |

## Planning: Circuits and groups

Before you turn a single screw, plan on paper:

1. **Count the circuits.** DIN 18015-2 specifies minimum equipment. Dedicated circuits are required for, for example, the **Stove**, **Washing machine**, **Dishwasher**, and **Tumble dryer**. Sockets and lighting are divided room by room.
2. **Plan for reserves.** Rule of thumb: 20 to 30 % of the pitch units should remain free. A full distribution board is a problem during the next renovation.
3. **Observe RCD requirements.** **DIN VDE 0100** requires an RCD with IΔn ≤ 30 mA for **sockets up to 32 A** and for **lighting circuits in apartments**. In practice, almost every Endstromkreis is behind an RCD today.
4. **Form groups.** Multiple LS-Schalter share one RCD. Distribute wisely: put lighting and sockets of the same room on **different** RCDs – if one trips, the light stays on. Six LS-Schalter per RCD is a proven upper limit.
5. **Distribute the Außenleiter** (phase conductors). Load L1, L2, and L3 as evenly as possible – the Kammschiene does this almost automatically.
6. **Check the Vorsicherung** (back-up fuse). The SLS-Schalter in the Zählerschrank (e.g., 35 A) must match the RCD (e.g., 40 A) so that the RCD is never overloaded.

## Step-by-step assembly

<Steps>

1. **Apply the Fünf Sicherheitsregeln** (five safety rules). **Freischalten** (disconnect), secure against reconnection, **Spannungsfreiheit feststellen** (verify absence of voltage), earth and short-circuit, cover adjacent live parts. Always – even for a "quick" look.

2. **Mount the distribution board.** At a comfortable operating height (usually between about 1.1 and 1.8 m), freely accessible, dry, not behind doors or furniture.

3. **Insert the Zuleitung.** Remove the sheath, bring the cores to length. Fine-stranded conductors require **Aderendhülsen** (wire end ferrules). The **Querschnitt** (cross-section) depends on the **Strom** (current), length, and installation method – NYM-J 5×10 mm² is common with a 35 A Vorsicherung, but you must calculate it every time.

4. **Install the Hauptschalter and Überspannungsschutz.** Connect the Zuleitung to the Hauptschalter, followed by the Überspannungsschutz. Keep its connection leads to L, N, and PE as short as possible, totaling under 0.5 m.

5. **Install and supply the RCDs.** From the Hauptschalter to each RCD: L1, L2, L3, and N. The N must pass **through** the RCD – this is the only way it can detect a Fehlerstrom.

6. **Connect the LS-Schalter with a Kammschiene.** For each RCD group, use a Kammschiene from the RCD output to the LS-Schalter. Cut the rail cleanly, no bare ends.

7. **Connect the outputs.** Connect the Außenleiter to the LS-Schalter output. Connect the Neutralleiter to the N-terminal of the **same** RCD group. Connect the Schutzleiter to the PE-terminal. Colors: **L1 brown, L2 black, L3 gray, N blue, PE green-yellow**.

8. **Tighten and test.** Use the torque specified by the manufacturer (often around 2 Nm for modular devices), then perform a pull test on every wire.

9. **Label.** Every switch gets its circuit on the labeling strip; the circuit directory goes inside the distribution board door.

10. **Perform the Erstprüfung** – see the next section. Without a **Prüfprotokoll** (test report), the installation is not finished.

</Steps>

## Erstprüfung according to DIN VDE 0100-600

Every new or modified installation is tested before commissioning. The Erstprüfung consists of three parts:

- **Visual inspection:** correct components, colors, labeling, covers, cable routing, no damage.
- **Testing:** function of protective devices, **Prüftaste** (test button) on the RCD, switching functions.
- **Measurement:**
  - **Continuity of the Schutzleiter** – every PE is connected with low resistance.
  - **Isolationswiderstand** (insulation resistance) – measured at 500 V DC, at least **1 MΩ** for installations up to 500 V.
  - **Schleifenimpedanz** (loop impedance) / **Kurzschluss** current – the LS-Schalter must safely disconnect in the event of a fault.
  - **RCD tripping** – at IΔn within **300 ms** at the latest; good devices trip much faster. Tripping current and tripping time are recorded in the Prüfprotokoll.
  - **Drehfeld** (phase sequence) – clockwise at the stove connection.

The result is a Prüfprotokoll with measured values, date, and signature. It is part of the handover.

## Common mistakes

- **Neutralleiter of two RCD groups connected.** The RCD sees a differential current and trips under load. The most common mistake in the Ausbildung.
- **Schutzleiter forgotten or loose.** Danger to life – and immediately visible during measurement.
- **Wrong characteristic or Nennstrom** (rated current). B16 on 1.5 mm² over long distances can be too much; B10 for lighting is the standard case.
- **Kammschiene cut incorrectly.** Bare ends next to the neighboring device are a short circuit waiting to happen.
- **Terminals not tightened.** Loose terminals get hot – fire hazard. Perform a pull test on every wire.
- **No labeling, no Prüfprotokoll.** Without both, the installation is a black box for the next electrician.
