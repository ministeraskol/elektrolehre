**wattwas** explains electrical engineering the way you need it in your Ausbildung (apprenticeship): short, with a schema, with a source – and in the language you think in best. For Azubis (apprentices), career changers, students, hobbyists, and everyone who wants to know what happens behind the Steckdose (socket).

## Why it exists

Most explanation sites for electrical engineering are only in German, but many Azubis speak Turkish, Arabic, Russian, or something else at home. wattwas keeps the **deutschen Fachbegriff (German technical term)** in every language – because you need it in the Prüfung (exam) – and explains everything around it in your language. In addition, there is **Leichte Sprache (easy language)** for everyone who is just learning German.

## What wattwas is not

Not a DIY guide. Only Elektrofachkräfte (qualified electricians) work on electrical systems; during your Ausbildung, always under supervision. Details in the [Disclaimer](/en/rechtliches/haftungsausschluss/).

## How wattwas is financed

- **No account, no tracking.** All texts are under CC BY-SA 4.0 – you are allowed to share and reuse them, with attribution.
- **Membership:** Anyone who [becomes a member](/en/mitglied/) gets new guides first and exam sprints via email – and helps decide what will be explained next.
- **Tool guide:** Recommendations may contain partner links in the future (marked with *). If you buy through them, wattwas receives a small commission – the price does not change for you. Reviews remain independent of this.

## Get involved

Found an error, a term is missing, or the language is clunky? Write a note via
[GitHub Issues](https://github.com/ministeraskol/elektrolehre/issues) – even without programming knowledge, one sentence is enough.
Native speakers who want to proofread a machine-translated page are particularly welcome.
