**wattwas** explains electrical engineering just the way you need it for your Ausbildung (vocational training): concise, with diagrams, with sources – and in the language you think in best. For apprentices, career changers, students, hobbyists, and anyone who wants to know what happens behind the Steckdose (power outlet).

## Why it exists

Most electrical engineering explanation sites are only in German, but many apprentices speak Turkish, Arabic, Russian, or something else at home. wattwas keeps the **German technical term** in every language – because you need it for your exams – and explains the context in your language. Additionally, there is **Easy Language** for everyone who is just starting to learn German.

## What wattwas is not

Not a DIY guide. Only qualified professionals should work on electrical systems; during your Ausbildung, always work under supervision. Details in the [Haftungsausschluss](/en/rechtliches/haftungsausschluss/).

## How wattwas is funded

- **No account, no tracking.** All texts are under CC BY-SA 4.0 – you are allowed to share and reuse them, provided you give credit.
- **Membership:** Anyone who [Mitglied wird](/en/mitglied/) gets new guides first and exam sprints via email – and helps decide what gets explained next.
- **Tool Guide:** Recommendations may contain partner links in the future (marked with *). If you buy through them, wattwas receives a small commission – the price does not change for you. Reviews remain independent of this.

## Get involved

Found a mistake, a term is missing, or the language is clunky? Send a note via [GitHub Issues](https://github.com/ministeraskol/elektrolehre/issues) – even without programming skills, one sentence is enough. Native speakers who want to proofread a machine-translated page are especially welcome.

Responsible in terms of press law: see [Impressum](/en/rechtliches/impressum/).
